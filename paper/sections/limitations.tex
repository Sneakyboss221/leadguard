\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\emph{One dataset.} All results come from PTB-XL, a single retrospective collection, used in its 100\,Hz version.
Transfer to other populations, devices, sampling rates and label sets was not tested, and results were not stratified
by sex or age.

\emph{Simulated, in-distribution failures.} Every failure is simulated, and every test failure type also occurs in
training with parameters from the same ranges; only the number of affected leads can exceed the training range. This
favours corruption-trained models and does not test robustness to failures that training did not anticipate. Removed
and flatlined leads are idealised and easy to detect. Real recordings fail in other ways, including intermittent
contact, motion artifacts with non-stationary spectra, mains interference and swapped cables, which give a
plausible but wrong signal \cite{batchvarov2007incorrect}. PTB-XL annotates baseline drift, static and burst noise
and electrode problems for some records \cite{ptbxl103}; we did not use these annotations, which would allow a test on
real artifacts.

\emph{No clean-trained gated models.} The gated and evidential variants were trained only with corruption, so the
study measures what these components add to corruption training, not what they contribute without it
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\emph{Quality labels from our own rules.} The assessor's targets are the severities of
Section~\ref{sec:data-failure}, which encode design choices such as treating wander with an amplitude ratio of one
or more as unreliable. Its high agreement shows that it learned these rules, not that it would judge real artifacts
as a clinician would. At the test SNR, EMG lies exactly on the reliability boundary, where agreement drops to
\sqaEmgTestCorrupted{} of the corrupted leads.

\emph{Seeds, budget and preprocessing.} Only the two directly compared models had three seeds; the ablation variants
are single runs, so differences of about 0.01 macro-AUROC between them cannot be ranked. No hyperparameters were tuned
and training was capped at 15 epochs, which leaves absolute performance below the published benchmark; stronger
models may change the size of the effects. Checkpoints were selected on clean validation data, and per-lead
standardisation removes absolute amplitude for all models alike, which affects voltage-based diagnoses such as
hypertrophy.

\emph{Statistical scope.} The bootstrap intervals are per condition, uncorrected for multiple comparisons, share test
records and do not include training variability.

\emph{One uncertainty method.} We evaluated one evidential formulation, trained only with corruption and without
temperature scaling, and did not compare ensembles or Monte Carlo dropout. Per-condition summaries of $s_p$ were not
recorded. The lack of response of $U$ to lead loss is therefore shown for this model only.

\emph{No clinical validation.} The system is a research prototype. The example record was selected from records on
which both models are correct with all leads, and nothing here has been validated clinically.
