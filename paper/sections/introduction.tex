\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
\input{sections/fig_overview}

\IEEEPARstart{T}{he} twelve-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) is the workhorse of cardiac diagnosis: ten electrodes,
twelve views of the heart's electrical activity, ten seconds of signal \citeneeded{ECG textbook or guideline on
the standard 12-lead ECG}. That picture assumes every electrode stays where it was placed. In emergency
departments, ambulances and intensive-care units it often does not. Electrodes peel away from sweating skin,
cables are tugged during transfers, muscle tremor buries the signal in noise, and leads end up swapped or
disconnected altogether \citeneeded{clinical studies on lead misplacement, detachment and artifact in acute
care}. The recording that reaches an automated classifier is frequently not the recording its training data
promised.

Deep networks trained on complete twelve-lead ECGs now rival cardiologists on curated benchmarks
\citeneeded{PTB-XL benchmark (Strodthoff et al.) and large-scale ECG DL studies}. Their failure mode under
missing or corrupted leads is less reassuring: a convolutional network has no built-in notion that a channel has
gone flat, so it simply produces a diagnosis from whatever it receives, and nothing in its output tells the
clinician that half of the recording was missing. Two families of remedies have been proposed. The first adds
\emph{spatial structure}: treat the leads as tokens, estimate each lead's quality and let attention ignore the
unreliable ones \citeneeded{lead-attention / missing-lead / masked-attention ECG works}. The second adds
\emph{uncertainty}: replace the softmax or sigmoid output by an evidential head that reports how much evidence
supports each prediction, so the system can abstain when it does not know \citeneeded{Sensoy et al.\ 2018 and
evidential ECG works}. Both are appealing, and both are usually evaluated as part of a larger system, next to a
change that may matter more: training on corrupted inputs.

We set out to test these mechanisms one at a time. We built a carefully controlled pipeline that combines both
(Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}): a frozen signal-quality assessor scores every lead, a mask-aware lead attention uses
those scores to suppress unreliable leads, and a per-class Beta-evidential head decides when to abstain. We then
trained nine model variants (the full system, three ablations and five baselines), all neural models under one
shared training budget, and evaluated them on identical, deterministically corrupted copies of the PTB-XL test
fold, with paired bootstrap intervals and oracle controls.

The outcome was not the one the architecture was designed for. Training with simulated lead failure, a simple
data augmentation, recovered most of the robustness that the clean-trained network lost; the quality gating and
the evidential head added nothing measurable on top. More strikingly, the evidential uncertainty did not respond
to the failures at all. As leads were removed, accuracy fell steadily while the model's reported uncertainty
stayed flat, so the network looked exactly as sure of a two-lead recording as of a complete one. We call this an
\emph{illusion of certainty}, and it is the main warning of this paper: an uncertainty estimate that is
well-behaved on clean data can be blind to the very failure it is meant to flag.

Concretely, our contributions are three controlled empirical findings on PTB-XL:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
  \item \textbf{Training with lead failure does the heavy lifting.} The same ResNet gains \gainDropKTen{}
        macro-AUROC with ten of twelve leads missing when trained on simulated failures, with no cost on complete
        recordings (Section~\ref{sec:res-dropout}).
  \item \textbf{Quality gating adds no measurable benefit.} Hard and soft gating by a learned per-lead quality
        score do not improve on un-gated lead attention, and the full system is never significantly better than a
        corruption-trained ResNet across \bootNRows{} paired comparisons; it is worse under baseline wander and
        heavy muscle noise (Section~\ref{sec:res-gating}).
  \item \textbf{Evidential uncertainty creates an illusion of certainty.} It gives the same selective-prediction
        risk as a plain confidence score, does not rise as leads disappear (\meanUKZero{} with all leads,
        \meanUKTen{} with two), and every abstention under lead loss comes from simply counting usable leads
        (Section~\ref{sec:res-edl}).
\end{itemize}

in the text is generated directly from the released result files.
