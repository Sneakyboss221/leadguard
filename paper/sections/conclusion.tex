\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether quality-gated lead attention and evidential abstention help an ECG classifier cope with lead
failure when its training already includes simulated failures. In this setting on PTB-XL, they did not measurably
help. Corruption training raised the macro-AUROC of the same ResNet by \gainDropKTen{} with ten leads missing. On top
of it, quality gating gave a provable invariance to excluded leads but no detectable gain in discrimination, and the
evidential uncertainty did not change as leads were removed although macro-AUROC fell from \aucLgEdlKZero{} to
\aucLgEdlKTen; only the lead-count rule triggered additional abstention.

Two points follow within the limits of this study. Architectures proposed for missing leads should be compared with
a baseline trained on the same failures, and an uncertainty estimate should be tested under the failures it is meant
to detect before it is relied on to detect them. Further work should use a factorial design with every architecture
trained with and without corruption, evaluate on real artifacts such as those annotated in PTB-XL and on failure
types held out of training, add a second dataset and more seeds for the ablation, and compare uncertainty methods
that are designed to respond to missing information.
