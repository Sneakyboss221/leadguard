\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All results are on the PTB-XL test fold (fold~10, \nTestRecords{} records). Every model sees the identical
corrupted test tensor for each condition (Section~\ref{sec:data}). Values for LG-EDL and the corruption-trained
ResNet are mean\,$\pm$\,standard deviation over three training seeds; all other variants were trained once and
are labelled \emph{single seed}. For random lead removal, each value is first averaged over the three random draws
of that condition. Paired bootstrap intervals compare LG-EDL with the corruption-trained ResNet on the same
resampled test records (\bootNBoot{} resamples; each side averaged over its three seeds).

\subsection{An illustrative case}
\label{sec:res-case}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_vignette.pdf}
\caption{One test patient, before and after the chest leads are unplugged. (a) The raw 12-lead recording; V1--V6
(grey) are removed in (c). (b, c) Class probabilities of the corruption-trained ResNet and of LG-EDL; ticks mark
each model's validation-tuned decision threshold. Lead quality comes from the frozen assessor. \emph{This case was
selected} from records that both models classify correctly with all leads; it illustrates the behaviour, it does
not measure how often it occurs (see Tables~\ref{tab:random}--\ref{tab:bootstrap}).}
\label{fig:vignette}
\end{figure*}

Fig.~\ref{fig:vignette} follows a single test patient, \vigPatient, whose recording carries one label, myocardial
infarction (MI). With all twelve leads, both models are right: the corruption-trained ResNet gives MI a probability
of \vigBaseMiFull{} and LG-EDL \vigLgMiFull. When the six chest leads are unplugged, the ResNet still detects the
infarction (\vigBaseMiCut), but its probability for ST/T change rises from \vigBaseStFull{} to \vigBaseStCut, past
its decision threshold of \vigBaseStThr, and it reports a second, false diagnosis. LG-EDL keeps a single, correct
label (MI \vigLgMiCut). Its uncertainty, however, hardly moves ($U$ from \vigLgUFull{} to \vigLgUCut) even though
half of the recording is gone, a first hint of the pattern quantified in Section~\ref{sec:res-edl}. Whether such
cases favour one model on average is the question the rest of this section answers; the short answer is that they
do not.

\subsection{Clean test performance}
\label{sec:res-clean}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Clean test performance. Mean\,$\pm$\,std over seeds where three seeds were trained; otherwise a single

\label{tab:clean}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=\columnwidth}\input{generated/tab_clean.tex}\end{adjustbox}
\end{table}

On complete recordings, architecture barely matters. The six convolutional models, the two ResNets and the four
lead-attention variants, fall within a narrow band of macro-AUROC, from \aucResCleanClean{} (ResNet,
clean-trained) to \aucResAttnClean{} (ResNet with un-gated lead attention, single seed)
(Table~\ref{tab:clean}). LG-EDL reaches \aucLgEdlCleanPM{}, the same as the corruption-trained ResNet (\aucResDropCleanPM{}); the
paired difference is \bootCleanDiff{} with 95\% CI \bootCleanCI{}. The patch Transformer (\aucTransClean{}),
XGBoost on per-lead statistics (\aucXgbClean{}) and the MLP (\aucMlpClean{}) are clearly weaker.

These clean scores are below the published PTB-XL benchmark for the same task at 100\,Hz, where the best single
model reaches \litBestSingleAuc{} and an ensemble \litEnsembleAuc{} \cite{strodthoff2021benchmark}. We tuned no
hyperparameters and trained for at most 15 epochs, so absolute performance is not the subject of this study; all
comparisons below are between models trained under the same budget.

Per class, the hardest class for every model is hypertrophy (HYP; LG-EDL \pcLgEdlHyp, corruption-trained ResNet
\pcResDropHyp) and the easiest is NORM (\pcLgEdlNorm{} and \pcResDropNorm). Calibration differs more than
discrimination: LG-EDL has an ECE of \eceLgEdlCleanPM, against \eceResDropCleanPM{} for the temperature-scaled
corruption-trained ResNet and between \eceResCleanClean{} and \eceResAttnClean{} for the other temperature-scaled
attention and ResNet variants.

\subsection{Finding 1: training with simulated lead failure provides most of the robustness}
\label{sec:res-dropout}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{fig1_auroc_vs_missing.png}
\caption{Macro-AUROC on the test fold as $k$ randomly chosen leads are removed (three draws per $k$). Shaded
bands: std over three seeds (LG-EDL and the corruption-trained ResNet only).}
\label{fig:random}
\end{figure*}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Macro-AUROC with $k$ leads removed at random (mean over three draws, then over seeds).}
\label{tab:random}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=\columnwidth}\input{generated/tab_random.tex}\end{adjustbox}
\end{table}

Removing leads separates the models far more than any architectural choice does
(Fig.~\ref{fig:random}, Table~\ref{tab:random}). The same ResNet trained on clean ECGs falls from
\aucResCleanKZero{} with all leads to \aucResCleanKTen{} when ten of twelve leads are removed, a loss of
\lossResCleanKTen. Trained with the simulated failures of Section~\ref{sec:data}, it falls only from
\aucResDropKZero{} to \aucResDropKTenPM, a loss of \lossResDropKTen; at $k=10$ the gap between the two training
regimes is \gainDropKTen{} macro-AUROC. The corruption-trained ResNet pays no price on clean data
(\aucResDropClean{} versus \aucResCleanClean). The clean-trained ResNet is a single run, but the gap at $k=10$ is
more than twenty times the seed standard deviation of its corruption-trained counterpart.

Once training includes lead failure, the remaining architectures barely differ. At $k=10$, LG-EDL
(\aucLgEdlKTenPM), LG-soft (\aucLgSoftKTen), LG-hard (\aucLgHardKTen), un-gated lead attention (\aucResAttnKTen) and
the corruption-trained ResNet (\aucResDropKTen) are close: the four attention variants span \ablRangeKTen{}
macro-AUROC, and the best of them is \gapAttnResKTen{} above the ResNet. Only the patch Transformer (\aucTransKTen), which mixes all leads in
every token, degrades noticeably more. XGBoost, which receives missing leads as missing feature values, falls
from \aucXgbKZero{} to \aucXgbKTen, and the MLP from \aucMlpKZero{} to \aucMlpKTen.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Macro-AUROC with a lead group removed, or with an artifact on six random leads (EMG at 10\,dB SNR,
baseline wander, amplitude clipping).}
\label{tab:group}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=\columnwidth}\input{generated/tab_group_artifact.tex}\end{adjustbox}
\end{table}

Losing a whole lead group hurts most where anatomy says it should (Table~\ref{tab:group}, Fig.~\ref{fig:group}).
The six precordial leads, V1--V6, wrap around the chest from the right sternal border to the left axillary line
and view the heart in the horizontal plane \cite{kligfield2007recommendations}. They are the primary diagnostic
window on the anterior wall, where an anterior myocardial infarction is recognised, and they carry much of the
ST-segment, T-wave and QRS-voltage evidence used for the other superclasses. A model that has only ever seen complete recordings has no reason to work without
them, and the clean-trained ResNet does not: its macro-AUROC collapses to \aucResCleanPrec, against
\aucResDropPrec{} after corruption training. The per-class breakdown shows that the damage reaches beyond
infarction. The clean-trained ResNet's AUROC for MI falls from \pcResCleanMi{} to \pcResCleanMiPrec, but its
AUROC for ST/T change falls further, from \pcResCleanSttc{} to \pcResCleanSttcPrec, and even NORM drops from
\pcResCleanNorm{} to \pcResCleanNormPrec; the corruption-trained ResNet keeps \pcResDropMiPrec, \pcResDropSttcPrec{}
and \pcResDropNormPrec{} for the same three classes. Artifacts tell the same story: with baseline wander on six
leads, the two training regimes reach \aucResCleanWanderSix{} and \aucResDropWanderSix.

\subsection{Finding 2: quality gating does not improve on un-gated lead attention}
\label{sec:res-gating}

\begin{table*}[t]
\centering
\caption{Ablation of the lead-attention family (all corruption-trained). Clean, random loss ($k=6$, $k=10$),
precordial loss (V1--V6) as macro-AUROC; clean ECE.}
\label{tab:ablation}
\input{generated/tab_ablation.tex}
\end{table*}

Telling the attention which leads to trust does not make it more robust than letting it attend to every lead
(Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). The four variants share the per-lead encoder and the attention code; they differ only
in whether attention and pooling are weighted by the estimated lead quality (hard mask or soft gate) and in the
output head. Across them, clean macro-AUROC spans \ablRangeClean{} and
macro-AUROC at $k=10$ spans \ablRangeKTen. The un-gated variant, which ignores lead quality entirely and receives
zero-filled leads like the CNN baselines, has the highest clean score (\aucResAttnClean) and is never the lowest
of the four under random or group lead loss. Three of the four variants are single runs, so differences of this
size cannot be ranked; for scale, the seed standard deviation of LG-EDL under random lead loss is at most
\seedStdLgEdlMaxLoss{} (Table~\ref{tab:random}). What the ablation does show is that gating provides no measurable benefit in this
setting.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Paired bootstrap of LG-EDL minus the corruption-trained ResNet (macro-AUROC; each side averaged over
three seeds; \bootNBoot{} resamples; percentile 95\% CI). $^{*}$: CI excludes zero. Random removal is reported
per draw; $k=0$ is identical to clean and omitted.}
\label{tab:bootstrap}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=\columnwidth}\input{generated/tab_bootstrap.tex}\end{adjustbox}
\end{table}

The paired bootstrap against the corruption-trained ResNet (Table~\ref{tab:bootstrap}) points the same way. Of
the \bootNRows{} comparisons, none has a confidence interval entirely above zero; \bootNSigNeg{} lie entirely
below zero, all of them artifact conditions: baseline wander on two, four and six leads (\bootWanderTwoDiff,
\bootWanderFourDiff, \bootWanderSixDiff) and EMG noise on six leads (\bootEmgSixDiff). The intervals are not
corrected for multiple comparisons and the conditions share test records, so they are not independent; with
\bootNRows{} comparisons at the 5\% level, about one false positive is expected even if the models were
equivalent. That all four significant differences are negative and concentrated in one artifact family suggests
a systematic effect, but we treat it as tentative.

One explanation is consistent with the ablation, although we did not test it directly. The quality assessor was
trained to label a lead with strong baseline wander as unreliable, so the gated models down-weight such leads even
though their QRS complexes remain readable, while the un-gated models still use them. Under wander on six leads,
the three gated variants (\aucLgHardWanderSix, \aucLgSoftWanderSix, \aucLgEdlWanderSix) score below both the
un-gated attention model (\aucResAttnWanderSix) and the corruption-trained ResNet (\aucResDropWanderSix).

Two further observations limit what the gating ablation can test. First, replacing the estimated quality with the
true quality ($q = 1-\text{severity}$) changes macro-AUROC by at most \oracleMaxAbsDiff{} for any gated run and
condition, so quality estimation is not the bottleneck. Second, the simulated failures are easy to detect:
removed leads are exactly zero or flat, the assessor scores untouched clean leads at \sqaMeanQClean{} on average,
and its lead-level agreement with the true reliability label is at least \sqaGate{} for every corruption type
outside the ambiguous band around severity 0.5. Its scores are therefore close to binary, which explains why hard masking
and soft gating behave almost identically. Our simulation exercises the masking mechanism but hardly the soft
weighting.

\subsection{Finding 3: evidential uncertainty neither improves selective prediction nor tracks lead loss}
\label{sec:res-edl}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{fig4_risk_coverage_calibration.png}
\caption{(a, b) Risk--coverage curves: records are kept in order of increasing uncertainty; risk is the mean
fraction of the five labels predicted wrongly at the validation-tuned thresholds. (c) Reliability diagram on the
clean test fold.}
\label{fig:riskcov}
\end{figure*}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Selective prediction: risk (mean per-record label error) at 50\%, 80\% and 100\% coverage, and the mean
risk over 20 coverage levels from 5\% to 100\% (lower is better). ``Confidence'' is
$1-\frac{1}{5}\sum_k \max(p_k, 1-p_k)$ computed from the model's own probabilities.}
\label{tab:selective}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=\columnwidth}\input{generated/tab_selective.tex}\end{adjustbox}
\end{table}

If the evidential head adds useful uncertainty, ranking records by its uncertainty $U$ should give lower risk at a
given coverage than ranking them by a simple confidence score computed from the same model's probabilities. It
does not (Table~\ref{tab:selective}, Fig.~\ref{fig:riskcov}). On clean data, LG-EDL with $U$ has a risk of
\riskLgEdlUCleanHalf{} at 50\% coverage, against \riskLgEdlConfCleanHalf{} with the confidence score; the mean
risk over all coverage levels is \aurcLgEdlUClean{} for both. With six leads removed the two are again
indistinguishable (\riskLgEdlUKSixHalf{} and \riskLgEdlConfKSixHalf{} at 50\% coverage). The lowest clean risk at
50\% coverage belongs to the un-gated lead-attention model with a plain confidence score (\riskResAttnConfCleanHalf).

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{LG-EDL abstention as leads are removed at random (mean over draws and seeds). The $U$ threshold $\tau$ is
the 95th percentile of $U$ on the clean validation fold (mean over seeds: \uThresholdMean).}
\label{tab:abstain}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=\columnwidth}\input{generated/tab_abstain.tex}\end{adjustbox}
\end{table}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_certainty.pdf}
\caption{An illusion of certainty. As leads are removed at random, (a) LG-EDL's macro-AUROC falls steadily, while
(b) its evidential uncertainty $U$ stays flat, far below the abstention threshold $\tau$. (c) Abstention by the
uncertainty rule therefore stays near the 5\% set on clean validation data; all additional abstention comes from
the lead-counting rule $\sum_j q_j<3$, which fires only once ten leads are gone. Lines: mean over three seeds (after
averaging the three random draws); bands: std over seeds.}
\label{fig:certainty}
\end{figure*}

Nor does $U$ respond to missing leads (Fig.~\ref{fig:certainty}, Table~\ref{tab:abstain}). We call this an
\emph{illusion of certainty}: the model's own estimate of how much it knows is, on average, the same for a complete
twelve-lead recording and for one reduced to two leads, although its accuracy is not. Its threshold was set so that 5\% of clean
validation records are rejected; on the test fold the $U$ criterion rejects \abstainUKZero\% of records with all
leads present and between \abstainUKTen\% and \abstainUKTwo\% for every $k$ up to ten, while the mean of $U$
drifts slightly \emph{down}, from \meanUKZero{} to \meanUKTen. All additional abstention comes from the second
rule, $\sum_j q_j < 3$: it never fires while four or more leads remain (mean $\Sigma q$ \meanQsumKEight{} at
$k=8$) and rejects every record at $k=10$ (\abstainQKTen\%). Under lead loss, the abstention behaviour of the
system is therefore that of a lead-counting rule, which needs neither the evidential head nor a learned quality
score for the failures simulated here. Finally, the evidential head is the least calibrated of the attention
variants (ECE \eceLgEdlCleanPM; Section~\ref{sec:res-clean}); we did not test whether temperature scaling would
close this gap.

\subsection{Summary of findings}
\label{sec:res-summary}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Training with simulated lead failure accounts for almost all of the robustness: \gainDropKTen{}
        macro-AUROC at $k=10$ for the same ResNet, with no cost on clean data.
  \item Given that training, neither hard nor soft quality gating improves on un-gated lead attention, and LG-EDL
        is never significantly better than the corruption-trained ResNet; it is significantly worse in
        \bootNSigNeg{} of \bootNRows{} artifact and lead-loss comparisons.
  \item The evidential uncertainty gives the same selective-prediction risk as a confidence score from the same
        model, does not increase as leads are removed, and is less calibrated than temperature-scaled
        sigmoid outputs; abstention under lead loss is driven entirely by counting usable leads.
\end{enumerate}
