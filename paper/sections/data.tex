\section{Data and Simulated Lead Failure}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{PTB-XL}
\label{sec:data-ptbxl}
We use PTB-XL version~1.0.3 \cite{wagner2020ptbxl,ptbxl103}, a public collection of clinical 12-lead ECGs of 10\,s
each, annotated with SCP-ECG statements; \ptbxlMalePct\% of its patients are male and \ptbxlFemalePct\% female. We use
the 100\,Hz release, so each record is a $12\times1000$ array in the lead order I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF, V1--V6. The
labels are the five diagnostic superclasses: normal ECG (NORM), myocardial infarction (MI), ST/T change (STTC),
conduction disturbance (CD) and hypertrophy (HYP). Every statement marked as diagnostic is mapped to its superclass
irrespective of its likelihood value, a record may carry several superclasses, and records without a diagnostic
superclass are excluded, which leaves \nRecordsKept{} records. We use the recommended stratified folds, which keep
all records of a patient in one fold \cite{wagner2020ptbxl,strodthoff2021benchmark}: folds 1--8 for training,
fold~9 for validation and fold~10 (\nTestRecords{} records) for testing. Each lead of each record is standardised to
zero mean and unit variance, and a lead with a standard deviation below $10^{-6}$ is set to zero. Standardisation
removes absolute amplitude, which matters for voltage criteria such as those for hypertrophy
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). Sex and age were not used as inputs.

\subsection{A Severity Model of Lead Failure}
\label{sec:data-failure}
All corruptions act on the standardised signal. Each takes a batch $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times12\times T}$ and
returns the corrupted signal with a per-lead severity $s_j\in[0,1]$, where $s_j=0$ means that lead $j$ is untouched
and $s_j=1$ that it carries no usable signal. The severity defines the training target of the quality assessor,
$q^\ast_j=1-s_j$, and a binary label: lead $j$ is \emph{reliable} if $s_j<0.5$. These rules are our design choices,
not clinical judgements. Five failure types are simulated (Fig.~\ref{fig:corruptions}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Removal}: the lead is set to zero ($s_j=1$), as when an electrode detaches.
  \item \emph{Flatline}: the lead holds its first sample for the whole record, as with a saturated amplifier
        ($s_j=1$). Because this happens after standardisation, the constant is generally not zero.
  \item \emph{Muscle (EMG) noise}: white noise band-limited to 20--45\,Hz in the Fourier domain and scaled to a
        target signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) relative to the lead's own power;
        $s_j=1-\mathrm{clip}(\mathrm{SNR}_{\mathrm{dB}}/20,0,1)$. Mains interference at 50 or 60\,Hz is not simulated
        because it lies at or above the Nyquist frequency of the 100\,Hz signal.
  \item \emph{Baseline wander}: a sinusoid with a frequency drawn from 0.1--0.5\,Hz, a random phase and an amplitude
        of $r$ times the lead's standard deviation, with $r$ drawn from 0.5--3; $s_j=\mathrm{clip}(r/2,0,1)$, so a lead
        is unreliable whenever $r\ge1$.
  \item \emph{Amplitude clipping}: saturation at a percentile of $|x|$ drawn from 70--95; $s_j$ is the clipped
        fraction of samples divided by 0.2, capped at~1.
\end{itemize}
All ranges are sampled uniformly.

\subsection{Training-Time Corruption}
\label{sec:data-train}
During training, corruption is sampled independently for every record of every batch. With probability 0.30, an
artifact (EMG with an SNR drawn from 0--20\,dB, baseline wander or clipping) is applied to one to three random
leads. With probability 0.50, zero to six random leads are removed, a quarter of them by flatlining instead of
zeroing. With probability 0.15, a whole lead group is removed: the precordial leads, the limb leads or V1--V3.
These events can combine; artifacts are applied before removals, and a lead affected twice keeps the larger
severity. If fewer than two reliable leads would remain, the least-corrupted leads are restored. Records with eight
or ten missing leads therefore occur in training only when a group removal and a random removal coincide.

\subsection{Test-Time Conditions}
\label{sec:data-test}
For evaluation, the test fold is corrupted once per condition on the CPU, with a random generator seeded by the
condition name and draw, so every model receives identical inputs. The 31 conditions are the clean test fold; random
removal of $k\in\{0,2,4,6,8,10\}$ leads with three draws each; removal of the precordial leads (V1--V6), the limb
leads (I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF) or V1--V3; and EMG at 10\,dB, baseline wander or clipping on two, four or six
random leads. Wander and clipping parameters are drawn from the training ranges and EMG at 10\,dB lies inside the
training range, so every test failure type also occurs in training; only the number of affected leads can exceed
what training usually contains. EMG at 10\,dB has a severity of exactly 0.5 and is labelled unreliable by the
narrowest margin; the condition was fixed before the experiments, and the quality assessor's agreement is reported
with and without such borderline leads (Section~\ref{sec:setup-metrics}).
