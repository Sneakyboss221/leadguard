\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why the simpler models held their ground}
The un-gated lead-attention model and the corruption-trained ResNet matched the full system under every form of
lead loss, and the un-gated model was slightly better on clean data. Three observations explain why quality gating
had so little to add.

\emph{The quality score carries no new information.} The assessor computes $q_j$ from the same corrupted signal
the classifier receives, so $\mathbf{q}$ is a deterministic function of $\mathbf{x}$. Gating can therefore only
change the inductive bias, not the evidence available. A network trained on corrupted records learns its own
implicit quality detector, and for the failures simulated here that task is easy: a removed lead is exactly zero
after standardisation, a flat line in every sense of the word.

\emph{Gating can discard usable signal.} The assessor was trained to call a lead with strong baseline wander
unreliable, yet wander is a slow drift on which the QRS complexes remain readable. Under wander on six leads all
three gated variants fall below the un-gated model and the ResNet (Section~\ref{sec:res-gating}), the pattern one
would expect if gating throws away information that an un-gated network still uses. This explanation is
consistent with our results but was not tested directly.

\emph{The soft gate is barely exercised.} Because the simulated failures are either clearly present or clearly
absent, the assessor's scores are nearly binary, and soft gating behaves like hard masking. Whether a graded
quality score helps with graded, subtle artifacts is a question our data cannot answer.

What gating does provide is a guarantee rather than accuracy: a lead below the floor provably cannot influence the
output (Section~\ref{sec:method-mala}). That property may matter for auditing and for explaining a decision to a
clinician, but it did not translate into better discrimination.

\subsection{Architectural complexity versus data augmentation}
The robustness on offer came almost entirely from one data-augmentation function. Training with simulated lead
failure improved the same ResNet by \gainDropKTen{} macro-AUROC at $k=10$; across the four lead-attention variants,
the whole quality-gating and evidential machinery spans \ablRangeKTen{}. The machinery is not free: it needs a
separately pre-trained assessor, a quality floor, an uncertainty threshold and a lead-count limit, and a training
run of LG-EDL took on average \secLgEdlRun{}\,s against \secResDropRun{}\,s for the corruption-trained ResNet,
about \timeRatioLgRes{} times longer, partly because the ResNets stopped early. The parameter counts are similar
(\paramsLgEdl{} against \paramsRes). For lead failure of the kind studied here, the cheaper intervention is also
the effective one, and any proposed architecture for missing leads should be compared against a baseline that was
trained with the same failures.

\subsection{The illusion of certainty: a cautionary tale}
The most consequential result concerns the uncertainty. As leads were removed, the macro-AUROC of LG-EDL fell from
\aucLgEdlKZero{} to \aucLgEdlKTen{}, while its mean uncertainty $U$ moved from \meanUKZero{} to \meanUKTen. A
clinician handed a two-lead strip would hedge; the network did not. The only safeguard that reacted was the
explicit rule that counts usable leads, and it reacted only once fewer than three leads' worth of signal
remained.

We offer a hypothesis for the mechanism, which our experiments did not isolate. Corruption training shows the
network the same diagnosis with and without its leads, and the evidential loss rewards confident, correct evidence
on every training record. Nothing in the objective asks the evidence to shrink when information is missing; if
anything, the model is taught to stay confident under lead loss, because that is what minimises the loss on the
corrupted training records. This matches the theoretical concern that evidential objectives do not incentivise
faithful epistemic uncertainty \cite{bengs2022pitfalls,shen2024mirage}. Testing it requires an evidential model
trained without corruption, or an objective that explicitly ties the evidence to the remaining signal.

The practical lesson is broader than this architecture. An uncertainty estimate that behaves sensibly on clean
data, here rejecting the intended 5\% of validation records, can be blind to exactly the failure it is meant to
catch. Uncertainty must be validated under the specific failures a deployed system will meet, and for
signal-level failures an explicit check on the signal itself is, at present, the more reliable alarm.

\begin{keybox}{Practical recommendations}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}\setlength{\leftmargin}{1em}
  \item Train every ECG classifier with simulated lead failure; it is cheap and gives most of the robustness.
  \item Compare any missing-lead architecture with a baseline trained on the same failures, never with a
        clean-trained one alone.
  \item Do not rely on a learned uncertainty score to detect lead failure without testing it under that failure;
        check the signal explicitly and abstain on too little usable signal.
\end{itemize}
\end{keybox}
