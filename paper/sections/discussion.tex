\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why Quality Gating Added Little}
Under random and group lead loss, the un-gated attention model and the corruption-trained ResNet performed as well
as the full system, and under baseline wander they performed better. Three properties of the setup are consistent
with this.

First, at test time the quality score adds no information about the input, because the assessor computes
$\mathbf{q}$ from the same corrupted signal that the classifier receives. The assessor was, however, trained with the
severity labels, which the classifiers never see, so gating does bring extra supervision into training; in our data
it did not help. A network trained on corrupted records can learn its own implicit quality detector, and for these
failures the task is easy, because a removed lead is exactly zero after standardisation and a flatlined lead is
constant.

Second, gating can discard usable signal. The assessor learned our rule that a lead with strong baseline wander is
unreliable, although the QRS complexes of such a lead remain readable. The lower scores of all three gated variants
under wander (Section~\ref{sec:res-gating}) fit this explanation, which we did not test, for example by retraining
the assessor with a different wander rule.

Third, the soft gate was hardly exercised. The simulated failures are either clearly present or clearly absent, the
assessor's scores are close to binary, and soft gating behaves like hard masking. Whether a graded quality score
helps with graded, subtle artifacts cannot be answered with these data.

What gating does provide is a guarantee that a lead below the floor cannot influence the output
(Section~\ref{sec:method-mala}). This property may be useful for auditing a model, but here it did not improve
discrimination.

\subsection{Corruption Training and Architecture}
Corruption training improved the same ResNet by \gainDropKTen{} macro-AUROC at $k=10$, whereas the four corruption-
trained attention variants span \ablRangeKTen. The gated system also costs more: it needs a separately trained
assessor, a quality floor, an uncertainty threshold and a lead-count limit, and a training run of LG-EDL took on
average \secLgEdlRun{}\,s against \secResDropRun{}\,s for the corruption-trained ResNet, partly because the ResNets
stopped earlier. The parameter counts are similar (\paramsLgEdl{} against \paramsRes).

These results show that, once corruption training is used, the gating and the evidential head add little. They do
not show that architecture is unimportant in general. The gated models were not trained without corruption, and
because masked leads cannot influence their output, such models might be partly robust to lead removal even
without it. Separating the contribution of the architecture from that of the training data requires a factorial
design in which every architecture is trained both with and without corruption. Until then, the conclusion that
the data support is narrower: an architecture proposed for missing leads should be compared with a baseline trained
on the same failures, because against a clean-trained baseline the effect of the training data is attributed to the
architecture.

\subsection{Evidential Uncertainty Under Lead Loss}
As leads were removed, LG-EDL's macro-AUROC fell from \aucLgEdlKZero{} to \aucLgEdlKTen{} while its mean $U$ stayed
between \meanUKTen{} and \meanUKZero. The only component that responded was the lead-count rule, and only when fewer
than three leads' worth of signal remained. LG-EDL's pooled calibration changed little up to eight missing leads,
but because $s_p$ was not summarised per condition, we cannot say whether a probability-based uncertainty would have
responded where $U$ did not.

Two explanations are possible, and neither was tested. Corruption training shows the network the same diagnosis with
and without many of its leads, and the evidential loss rewards confident, correct evidence on every training
record; nothing in the objective asks the evidence to shrink when information is missing. This agrees with the
theoretical concern that evidential objectives do not reward faithful epistemic uncertainty
\cite{bengs2022pitfalls,shen2024mirage} and with the observation that uncertainty estimates degrade under shift
\cite{ovadia2019trust}. In addition, the weighted-mean pooling of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pool} keeps the scale of the pooled
representation independent of the number of remaining leads, so the head receives no direct signal of how much input
was lost. If the first explanation is correct, the training that provides the robustness may also make $U$
uninformative about lead loss. An evidential model trained without corruption, or an objective that ties the
evidence to the remaining signal, would test this. Until such results exist, an uncertainty estimate intended to
flag lead failure should be validated under that failure, and for failures visible in the signal itself, an explicit
signal check such as the lead-count rule was the more dependable safeguard in our experiments.
