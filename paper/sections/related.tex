\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Classification From Reduced and Missing Leads}
The PhysioNet/Computing in Cardiology Challenge 2021 asked whether reduced-lead recordings retain the diagnostic
information of the full twelve leads \cite{reyna2021willtwodo}. The winning entry, an ensemble of residual
convolutional networks with multi-head attention, ranked first in every lead configuration, with scores on the
hidden validation set that changed little from twelve to two leads \cite{nejedly2021cinc,nejedly2022ensemble}. Oh
\emph{et al.} pre-train ECG encoders with random lead masking so that the learned features do not depend on a fixed
lead set \cite{oh2022leadagnostic}, and in multimodal learning, ModDrop trains networks with whole input channels
dropped at random so that they keep working when a modality is missing \cite{neverova2016moddrop}. In these works
the model is told, or trained to expect, that some inputs are absent. In our setting the input is always a
twelve-lead array and a failed lead arrives as zeros, a constant or noise; the question is whether an explicit
quality estimate adds anything once training already includes such failures.

\subsection{ECG Signal Quality Assessment}
Signal quality indices were developed to decide whether an ECG can be interpreted and to suppress false alarms.
The PhysioNet/Computing in Cardiology Challenge 2011 asked for algorithms that judge, while the patient is still
present, whether a 12-lead ECG recorded with a mobile phone is of acceptable quality \cite{silva2011challenge}.
Clifford \emph{et al.} combined several quality indices to classify the clinical acceptability of 12-lead ECGs
\cite{clifford2012sqi}, and Behar \emph{et al.} used quality indices to reduce false arrhythmia alarms
\cite{behar2013sqi}. These methods assess a recording or a lead as a separate step. Our quality assessor is a small
learned per-lead network trained on simulated corruption, and its output is used inside the classifier, to weight
attention and pooling, rather than to accept or reject a recording.

\subsection{Benchmarks on PTB-XL}
Strodthoff \emph{et al.} established the reference benchmark for PTB-XL with its recommended patient-level folds;
ResNet- and Inception-based convolutional networks performed best across tasks \cite{strodthoff2021benchmark}. We use
the same folds, label definition and 100\,Hz signal. Our models are trained under a small shared budget and do not
reach the benchmark's absolute scores (Section~\ref{sec:res-clean}), so all comparisons in this paper are between
models trained identically.

\subsection{Uncertainty Under Distribution Shift}
Ovadia \emph{et al.} compared uncertainty methods under dataset shift and found that the quality of predictive
uncertainty degrades as the shift increases \cite{ovadia2019trust}. Evidential deep learning places a Dirichlet
distribution over class probabilities, treats the network outputs as evidence and reads uncertainty from the total
evidence, all from one deterministic network \cite{sensoy2018edl}; its original evaluation reported good
out-of-distribution detection and robustness to adversarial perturbation. Bengs \emph{et al.} showed that the loss
functions used to train such second-order predictors do not give them an incentive to represent epistemic
uncertainty faithfully \cite{bengs2022pitfalls}, and Shen \emph{et al.} extended the analysis to many evidential
objectives, finding that the learned epistemic uncertainty does not vanish even with infinite data
\cite{shen2024mirage}. Simulated lead failure is a shift whose type and severity are controlled exactly, and
removing a lead cannot add information about the diagnosis, which makes it a direct test of whether an evidential
uncertainty responds to lost information.

\subsection{Calibration and Selective Prediction}
A single temperature fitted on held-out data is a simple and effective calibration method for modern networks
\cite{guo2017calibration}. Selective classification lets a classifier abstain in order to reach a target risk, and
thresholding the network's own maximum class probability is a strong rejection rule \cite{geifman2017selective}. The
probability-based score used here (Section~\ref{sec:setup-metrics}) is a multi-label version of that rule.
